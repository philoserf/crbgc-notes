The Walking Golfers Society is a society organized around a single sentence—"the game is meant for walking," borrowed from Michael Murphy's Shivas Irons—and we admire the economy of that. It exists to make the case for walking the course on the grounds of health, sociability, pace, and the simple experience of the round, and to encourage golfers to leave the cart behind. There is something bracing about a club with exactly one plank in its platform, defended without apology. The C&RBGC, whose own platform is not much longer, finds it congenial.

The cause's most visible home today is [WalkingGolfer.com](https://walkinggolfer.com/), which Rob Rigg relaunched in 2025 with backing from Stewart Golf—more a directory of course-walkability ratings than a calendar of events—while a free-to-join Society side persists and the [[Society of Hickory Golfers]] keeps a cross-reference of its own. The walking and the hickory arguments are the same argument approached from different ends, both insisting that the friction the modern game has engineered away was doing useful work. We count it kin for the conviction, and for the format—a club that takes one small true thing and refuses to let it go.
