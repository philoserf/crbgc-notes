The process to get a rating is fairly straightforward, and Sparta Moose is an entirely normal candidate for an official rating. The Golf Association of Michigan (GAM) is the USGA-authorized association responsible for rating courses in Michigan. Nine-hole courses qualify as long as they exceed 750 yards; Sparta Moose at roughly 2,764 yards easily clears that requirement. 

The practical sequence would be:

1. The Lodge or course management contacts GAM. This needs to come from the facility rather than an individual golfer. GAM specifically says that a course needing a new rating should contact its Course Rating Department as soon as possible. The current contact listed is Hunter Koch, GAM Director of Course Rating, at [hkoch@gam.org](mailto:hkoch@gam.org) or [248-478-9242 ext. 118](tel:248-478-9242;118). 
2. Determine whether Sparta Moose is already a GAM member club. If it is, the Lodge can submit GAM's Course Rating Application and select "New Course / Never Rated." If it is not a GAM member club, GAM asks it first to submit the New Club Member Application. New member clubs without a valid rating are required to obtain one during their first year of membership. 
3. Tell GAM exactly what is to be rated. The application asks for the number of holes, number of tees to be rated for men and women, anticipated readiness date, and the reason for the rating. Sparta Moose could have each existing tee rated. GAM can also establish ratings for combination or hybrid tees if the Lodge wants them. 
4. GAM schedules the fieldwork. Nine-hole courses actually get a somewhat simpler treatment than 18-hole courses. GAM says a nine-hole course is normally measured and rated completely in one day, using a team of roughly four to eight trained raters. 
5. The course is precisely measured. GAM does not simply accept the yardages on the scorecard. Its team establishes the official length of every hole from permanent measurement points for every tee being rated. GPS and/or laser surveying methods are used. Tee markers need to be positioned where they represent ordinary day-to-day play. 
6. The raters evaluate every hole. They examine effective playing length plus the standard USGA obstacle factors: topography, fairway characteristics, green targets, rough and recoverability, bunkers, crossing obstacles, lateral obstacles, trees, green surfaces, and related factors. They evaluate the course from both scratch-golfer and bogey-golfer perspectives. 
7. They normally play the course afterward. For a nine-hole rating, GAM describes the day as measurement in the morning, data entry and preparation of rating documents, field rating later in the morning, followed by lunch and golf. The playing round lets the team check observations from the perspective of an actual player. 
8. GAM processes and issues the official ratings. The result is a USGA Course Rating and Slope Rating for the applicable tees. GAM also provides verified yardages and can provide recommended stroke-index allocations. The rating then remains valid for ten years, barring significant course changes. 

There is a cost issue, but it doesn't appear formidable. Existing GAM member clubs in good standing receive their normal periodic rating service without a separate rating charge. A new GAM member course pays a setup fee and prepays two years of club membership dues before GAM schedules the rating. GAM describes the setup charge as "nominal" rather than publishing a fixed amount. The club is also asked to provide course access, carts as necessary, and a modest lunch for the volunteer raters. 

The wrong first move is to assemble technical information or measure the course independently. The first useful act is for a Lodge officer or whoever manages the golf course to contact Hunter Koch at GAM and say essentially: "We operate a nine-hole course at Grand Rapids–Sparta Moose Lodge No. 50 that apparently has no current Course Rating or Slope Rating, and we'd like to investigate having it rated."

GAM can then determine whether the Lodge is already in its club system, whether some forgotten historical rating exists in its records, what membership/setup cost would apply, and when a rating team could come out.

It is worth pursuing. A rating would convert Sparta Moose from a course where handicap golfers effectively play "off the books" into one where nine-hole scores can participate normally in the World Handicap System. It would also produce something historically interesting: an objective modern measure of the difficulty of the surviving nine holes of the old Camp Lake course, whose story is told in [[Camp Lake Golf Course History]].
