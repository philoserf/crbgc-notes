The current public record gives a reasonably good picture of the lodge organization, but a surprisingly poor picture of who presently manages the golf course itself.

GR/Sparta Moose Lodge #50 does not appear to operate the golf course as a separate golf-club corporation. The Lodge itself owns and maintains the nine-hole course as one of its facilities. Algoma Township describes the organization simply as a Moose lodge maintaining both a lodge building and a private nine-hole golf course.  That distinction matters: the ultimate authority over the course appears to lie with Lodge No. 50 rather than with an independent golf board, PGA professional, or outside management company.

The Lodge is a fairly substantial organization. Its most recent publicly available federal filing, for the fiscal year ending April 2025, reports 24 employees, about \$773,000 in revenue, about \$763,000 in expenses, and assets of roughly \$2.03 million. The filing identifies David Feder as Administrator.  There is evidence, however, that the administrator changed around that period. Moose International's 2025 Leadership Award list identifies Leah Ruuti as Administrator of Grand Rapids–Sparta Lodge No. 50, and Moose convention records also identify Ruuti as the lodge's representative. Her current professional profile associates her with Lodge No. 50 as well. I would therefore regard Leah Ruuti as the best-supported current lodge administrator, with Feder probably representing the preceding reporting period. 

The golf side is murkier.

One currently indexed golf directory identifies Sean Smith as course manager.  Smith is certainly a real and continuing figure in the Michigan Moose organization: the Michigan Moose Association currently lists him as its State Secretary.  But I would not yet assume that he is the active day-to-day manager of the Sparta course. The golf-directory record could easily be an old entry that has simply survived unchanged.

That problem becomes obvious with another directory. It lists Ted Attwood as course manager and club professional and Terry Scott as superintendent.  Those data are almost certainly historical rather than current. Terry Scott is independently documented as the Sparta Moose golf-course superintendent in 1998, and the same directory gives \$12.50 green fees—an obvious indication that its management information originated decades ago.  Ted Attwood remains associated with Grand Rapids–Sparta Moose in Michigan Moose records, so the name itself is not spurious, but I would not treat the stated job titles as current. 

There is at least one other person publicly associated with the golf operation. Jilliann Dominiak describes herself as a long-term Lodge volunteer whose roles include Golf Course Events Coordinator and co-coordinator of the Hook A Kid On Golf program. That is self-reported rather than an official organizational roster, but it suggests that some golf functions are handled through member volunteers and committees rather than through a conventional country-club golf department. 

The overall operating model therefore looks more like a fraternal lodge that happens to own and operate a golf course than a conventional private golf club. I find no reliable current evidence of a PGA professional, director of golf, general manager devoted solely to golf, or presently identified professional superintendent. A current golf listing explicitly gives "Golf Pro: N/A."  The course is member-and-guest oriented, has carts and club rentals, but no driving range, and does not appear to operate the sort of public tee-time/pro-shop infrastructure typical of a commercial course. 

That begins to explain some of the other things we have found. The lack of a USGA/GAM rating, sparse official course information, conflicting historical yardages and opening dates, and difficulty identifying a current superintendent all make sense if golf is being operated as an amenity of the Lodge rather than as a separately professionalized golf business.

For the question we're really circling—who could actually cause the course to be rated and perhaps improve its golf administration?—I would presently map the authority roughly this way:

Lodge board/officers → Lodge administrator (apparently Leah Ruuti) → whoever currently has responsibility for the golf course/grounds → golf volunteers or committee.

I would not approach Sean Smith, Ted Attwood, or anyone listed as superintendent on the strength of the web directories alone. The cleanest next piece of research would be to establish the 2026–27 Lodge officers and committee structure, particularly whether there is a Golf Committee, Golf Chairman, Grounds Committee, or current course superintendent. That would tell us who actually has both responsibility and authority over the course today.
